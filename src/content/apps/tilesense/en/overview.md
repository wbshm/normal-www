## Why discard this tile?

Recognizing the tiles is just the beginning. Which one should you discard? How far is your hand from a ready hand? Which draws would improve it? TileSense helps you work through those choices.

Start with a short lesson and try a few questions. When you miss an answer, a visual explanation uses tiles and color to show you why. A few minutes a day can turn a hunch into a choice you understand.

## From the basics, one step at a time

**7 units and 34 lessons** cover the tiles, chow, pung and kong, then shanten, effective tiles and partial melds, before moving on to defense, push-or-fold and calls.

Lessons also explain rule differences, including seven pairs, thirteen orphans, Sichuan's void suit and tile swap, and the Guobiao minimum score. Build a foundation in tile efficiency, then explore how different rules change your decisions.

## Put what you learn into practice

- **Today's practice**: 5 questions, about 2 minutes, for a small daily routine.
- **Skill check**: 10 discard questions to test your judgment and chart your progress.
- **Focused drills**: practice choosing a discard, counting shanten or finding effective tiles.
- **Mistake book**: missed questions are saved automatically and removed when you answer them correctly in review.

Answers are checked by a tile-efficiency engine, with visual explanations to help you understand the reasoning.

## Try a hand of your own

Enter a hand and compare the shanten and effective tiles for each possible discard. Mark tiles you have already seen on the table to compare the draws that are still available.

The analyzer supports **13-tile and Taiwanese 16-tile hands**. Optional special hands, including seven pairs and thirteen orphans, can be enabled in the analyzer's rule settings.
